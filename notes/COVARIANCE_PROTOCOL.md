# Covariance and reward audit — fixed protocol, 7 October 2026

Recorded before inspecting the extension's investment outcomes. Preserve all earlier experiments.

Primary design: N=10,30,49 French value-weighted industry portfolios; existing August 2026 vintage; 1990–2025 evaluation (432 months); past-only L=120; sample excess means; phi=5; 10bp per unit L1 turnover; long-only simplex. No parameter selection from new outcomes.

Cross six covariance estimators with target/raw/scalar/risk-only/joint/minimum-variance controls: sample, original fixed 10% diagonal shrinkage, Ledoit–Wolf data-driven scaled-identity shrinkage (2004 JMA), Ledoit–Wolf constant-correlation shrinkage (2004 JPM; inspected 2003 working-paper Appendix A/B), one observed market factor, and three observed Fama–French factors (Mkt-RF/SMB/HML). Factors already exist in the archived RF source. Covariance scale is unbiased-sample units throughout: ML shrinkage estimates multiplied by L/(L-1). Factor OLS includes an intercept; factor sample covariance uses L-1, diagonal residual variance uses L-K-1. This is an explicitly documented adaptation of Fan/Fan/Lv (2008), not an exact reproduction of its no-intercept residual convention. No nonlinear shrinkage or POET claim.

Within-estimator common states are that estimator's target pre-trade holdings. Measure distance and fitted regret against its own target. Own-state paths measure realised net MV, Sharpe, volatility, turnover, drawdown. In a separate common original-diagonal-target state, measure how changing covariance changes the target, and evaluate the covariance perturbation bound. This avoids treating an estimator change as reward misalignment. No unknown population historical covariance is asserted.

Primary uncertainty: paired circular 12-month-block bootstrap, 1000 resamples, seed 8128, joint versus raw within each estimator and target versus original-diagonal target across estimators. Use one family of 15 cross-estimator target differences for a simultaneous max-deviation band as a sensitivity check; marginal intervals alone do not establish a selected winner. Include three predeclared calendar blocks (1990–2007, 2008–2015, 2016–2025). Report all methods, not just the best.

Additional reward comparator: explicitly restricted adaptation of Habibnia/Soltanzadeh (2024), Eq.8/Table2. Long-only unit deployed capital; assetwise positive/negative monthly total-return PnL; penalty=25; reward cost coefficient=25/5 times the common turnover cost; no borrowing, shorts, SAC or CNN-MHA reproduction. Also test penalty=1 and 5 as declared sensitivities. Do not call a departure from MV a defect in a reward intended for downside preferences. Analyse its covariance blindness only under this stated restriction.

Finite calibration diagnostic: anchor mean from previous holdings and from zero, solve the jointly scaled objective, certify target gap <= phi*(anchor-target mean)^2 + A*own-objective numerical certificate, A>0. Generalise proof to deterministic convex financing/turnover costs; test a bounded long/short/cash model with asymmetric borrowing spread. Distinguish this controlled numerical example from empirical replication of the crypto lending environment.

Known-covariance simulation: 30 fixed seeds, N=10,49,100; L=60,120; three observed Gaussian factors, independent and block-correlated residual designs. Keep population mean known to isolate covariance effects; compare sample, fixed diagonal, both linear shrinkages, market-only, FF3-style factor. For N>=L allow semidefinite sample covariance for constrained target/raw/joint solves; do not invert it for the scalar rule. Report covariance error, true-objective regret and within-estimate reward alignment. This is synthetic evidence, distinct from historical returns and not evidence of a universally best estimator.

## Sequential extension: one auxiliary update

After the 18 primary cases and January anchor diagnostic were complete, add one explicitly exploratory comparator without selecting parameters: initialise the MVPI mean auxiliary at m' times current pre-trade holdings and take exactly one portfolio update. It requires no precomputed target. Evaluate common-state alignment and its own walk-forward path for all six estimators and all three universes, using every original setting unchanged. Credit this as a one-step MVPI adaptation, not a new iterative algorithm. Retain all prior outcomes, document the sequential design, and do not include it in a prespecified superiority claim. This tests the practical implication of the proved approximate-anchor bound.
