# Historical study protocol — fixed before inspecting strategy outcomes

Date: 26 September 2026. Dataset: Kenneth French ten value-weighted industry portfolio monthly total returns and Fama–French risk-free monthly returns. Retrieve original ZIPs with hashes. Use complete months through December 2025 only. Estimation starts January 1970; first evaluation January 1990. This is a retrospective walk-forward study on a current revised data vintage, not a point-in-time constituent database or live executable fund.

Primary question: does unconstrained scalar second-moment calibration preserve specified preferences once allocations are constrained and turnover is penalised? Secondary: how do reward-optimal policies perform on subsequent observed returns? Historical conditional mean/covariance estimates are not an oracle for the real market. The exact historical alignment reference is a fitted one-period mean–variance problem, not a known true multiperiod equilibrium.

Long-only fully invested ten-industry allocations. Rolling 120 months, 10% diagonal covariance shrinkage, no mean shrinkage in primary run. All inputs for month t use months strictly before t. Primary absolute risk penalty phi=5 and turnover penalty 10bp per unit of risky L1 turnover. Historical net returns use the explicitly first-order convention gross return minus that proportional turnover charge. Pre-trade weights drift with the previous month's gross asset returns; all strategies start equal-weighted. This conventional cost approximation does not model intramonth execution or market impact.

Compare: covariance-MV reference; raw second-moment phi; unconstrained-calibrated phi(1−nu); constraint-aware penalty-only calibration; joint risk/cost calibration; adapted MVPI with jointly optimised mean auxiliary; log utility and log-minus-variance reward (exact paper locator to be recorded before attributing); equal weights; minimum variance. Every method uses the same feasible set, estimates and dates. Direct objectives are solved to a numerical certificate. No historical superiority will be inferred from exact model alignment.

Evaluation: fitted-target objective gap and allocation distance evaluated on a COMMON pre-trade reference state; separate own-state historical wealth paths, arithmetic return/volatility, monthly MV score, annualised excess Sharpe, maximum drawdown and turnover. Report paired moving-block bootstrap intervals for differences (12-month blocks, 1000 resamples, fixed seed). Main figures use the primary setting; robustness phi={2,5,10}, cost={0,10,25}bp; estimation windows={60,120,240}, shrinkage={0,.1,.5}; avoid choosing a winning setting after results. Chronological subperiods 1990–2007, 2008–2015, 2016–2025. A second industry grouping can test external asset-universe sensitivity if accessible.

Learning is a separate finite-budget approximation experiment. Train an explicit policy/optimizer on training observations only, compare with its own exact reward optimum, and distinguish training variation from uncertainty in the historical sample. A neural solver benchmark is not described as a reproduction of a published full architecture.

Finite-budget diagnostic fixed before running: independent ten-way softmax logits for each of the 432 historical common states, Adam lr=.05, logits N(0,.1²), seeds0–4, budgets50/250/1000/5000. Optimise target/raw/joint rewards with identical initialisations and budgets. Inputs are each state's lagged moments and reference pre-trade weights, never its realised next return. This deliberately unrestricted contextual policy isolates numerical optimisation error; it is not a cross-state predictive neural agent or reproduction of DPG/DDPG/PPO. Report own-reward suboptimality separately from the signed target-value decomposition; do not add two differently measured gaps.
